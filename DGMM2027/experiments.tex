\noindent\textbf{Experimental evaluation.}
We evaluate the local measures on synthetic 3D digital data.
For each point $p$ and radius $r$, let
$W=(W_0/r^0,\ldots,W_d/r^{2d})$ denote the normalized local measures.
We compare $W$ with the theoretical values $\bar W(k)$
of a $k$-dimensional unit ball, using
\[
d_{k,j}:=
\begin{cases}
(W_j-\bar W_j(k))/\bar W_j(k), & j\le k,\\
W_j/\bar W_j(j), & j>k,
\end{cases}
\quad
q_k:=\exp\left(-\sum_{j=0}^d j\,d_{k,j}^2\right).
\]
We define the dimension scores by $P_k=q_k/\sum_{\ell=0}^d q_\ell$.
\RefFigure{fig-dimension-analysis} illustrates these scores on a
synthetic 3D digital set combining structures of different intrinsic
dimensions. The results show clear dimension discrimination, little
sensitivity to orientation, and good visual stability under Kanungo
noise.  Processing 80k points takes approximately $1$s on an Apple M2
Max laptop (including nearest neighbor data structure construction,
computing local volume projections and scores $(P_k)_{k=0,1,2,3}$).

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}
We introduced projection-based geometric invariants
characterizing the intrinsic dimension of submanifolds
of $\R^d$, with quantitative stability bounds under
cubical digitization. Experiments illustrate their
practical effectiveness, including under noise.
Individual projection volumes also provide estimates
of the absolute normal components of hypersurfaces.

\noindent\textbf{Perspectives.}
Future work includes extending stability to other
discretization schemes (GIQ, OBQ, and Hausdorff
discretizations), recovering oriented normals from
surfel orientations, and developing robust multiscale classifiers
for local dimension, boundary detection, and other
geometric features.
