\noindent\textbf{Experimental evaluation.} We evaluate the robustness
of the local measures on synthetic 3D digital data. For a point $p$,
let $W:=(W_0/r^0,\ldots,W_d/r^{2d})$ be its normalized local measures
for a radius $r$. Then define
\[
d_{k,j} := \left\{
\begin{array}{l}
  (W_j - \bar{W}_j(k))/\bar{W}_j(k))~\text{when $0\Le j \Le k$}\\
  W_j/\bar{W}_j(j)~\text{when $k < j \Le d$}
\end{array}
\right.,
\qquad
q_k := \Pi_{j=0}^d e^{-j d_{k,j}^2}.
\]
The value $q_k$ is the greatest when the vector $W$ ressembles the
vector $\bar{W}(k)$. Then the probability that $p$ has dimension $k$
can be expressed as $\mathrm{P}[\dim(p)=k] := \mathrm{softmax}_k(q_0,
\ldots, q_d)$. \RefFigure{fig-dimension-analysis} illustrates these
values on a toy 3D image assembling digitized submanifolds of
different dimensions. Note how the probabilities are invariant to the
orientation of digitized manifolds, while staying robust to noise. The
computation time is about $1$s for 80k points.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}  We have presented new geometric
invariants that are characteristic of the intrinsic dimension of
submanifolds of $\R^d$ and remain robust to their digitizations, with
proven quantitative error bounds. By essence, they are robust to noise
and even outliers, and simple to compute. Furthermore, we have shown
that projection volumes are also related to the normal vector field
for hypersurfaces.

\noindent\textbf{Perspectives.} On the theoretical side, we wish to
extend the stability result to other standard discretization processes
like GIQ, OBQ or

Hausdorff discretization. On digitized surfaces, we could exploit the
orientation of surfels to orient the normal estimator
$\widehat{n}_h(p,r)$, hence proving its multigrid convergence. On a
more practical level, we intend to develop a classifier that takes as
input the multiscale values $W_m(p,r)$, and outputs the dimension, the
probability that $p$ lies on the boundary, or other geometric
features.
